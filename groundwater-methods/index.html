California groundwater · Methods

What a dry well costs: methods and sources

How the figures in the California groundwater series were built. Last checked 1 October 2026.

← Back to the series
How to read the tags. Documented a source states the figure. Derived my arithmetic on sourced numbers, shown. Estimate a market or illustrative figure, not a household report. Costs are per household. Dry-well counts are self-reported to the State and are a floor, not a full count.

1. Dry-well counts

The counts come from the State of California's public dry-well reporting system (Department of Water Resources). Households report their own failures voluntarily, so every count is a floor.

Derived I analysed the public export downloaded in May 2026: 6,139 household water shortage reports, of which 5,891 are dry wells. By current status, 2,954 are outages (50%), 1,047 have an interim solution (18%), 1,880 are resolved (32%) and 10 are listed as vulnerable.

Derived The time charts place each report in the month its problem began. 1,573 dry-well reports (27%) have no start date and are left out of those charts, so the charts understate the total. The largest month on record is July 2021, with 283 reports.

2. How costs are split into three stages

StageMeaningWho paysUnit
UnresolvedNo state help yet, so the household covers its own water.The householdPer month
InterimTemporary water supplied by the state or its partners while a household waits for a permanent fix: bottled water, a tank filled by truck, or both.The statePer month
ResolvedA new well or a connection to a public water system.Usually the stateOne-time, per well

The units differ between stages, so the three numbers are not additive.

3. Unresolved: about $100 to $600 a month, paid by the household

Low end

  • Documented $80 a month for bottled water for drinking and cooking, El Adobe (Circle of Blue, 2021). This is a contamination case, not a dry well, and the household still has tap water for hygiene.
  • Documented The State's cost model assumes $1.25 a gallon and 60 gallons a month ($75) plus $22 a month for delivery, about $97 a month (2024 Cost Assessment, supplemental appendix). This is a program cost, not household spending.
  • The $100 low end rounds these two figures up.

High end

No single source reports $600. It is the sum of documented costs for a household that has lost its well entirely:

  • Documented Bottled water: $80 to $97 a month (above).
  • Documented Trucked water: about $200 a month for 5,000 gallons, Clovis area (CNBC, 2015; haulers charged $200 to $500 per truckload), and about $350 a month, Paso Robles area, three dry wells and a 500-gallon tank (SLO Tribune via Yahoo).
  • Documented Laundromat: $30 per weekly visit, about $130 a month, Glenn County dry-well resident (CalMatters, 2021).
  • Documented Gym memberships for showers: $10 a month (USDA Rural Development).
  • Derived $80 to $97 + $200 to $350 + $130 = about $410 to $580; with gym memberships, about $600.

Caveats

  • The sources span 2015 to 2024 and different regions (Clovis, Paso Robles, Glenn County, El Adobe).
  • The range shows what families spent, not what they needed. Hygiene water costs are poorly documented.
  • Informal sharing, such as a neighbour's hose or relatives hauling water, is documented in the USDA and Food & Water Watch accounts but without costs.
  • Tank hardware is excluded because the State normally supplies tanks in the interim stage. Households that are ineligible (renters, for example) or still waiting may buy their own; a generic installed estimate is $1,620 to $3,170 (Angi) Estimate.

4. Interim: about $55 to $1,100 a month, paid by the state

Households typically pay nothing at this stage. DWR's Water Tank Program provides a 2,500-gallon tank, delivery and installation, a small pump and plumbing, and hauled water. DWR publishes no per-household price for the tank.

Low end, bottled water only

Derived Self-Help Enterprises' regional table (FY 2026-27 Fund Expenditure Plan, Appendix N, Table N-1) lists $1,740,312 across 2,681 bottled-water households, about $649 per household. $649 / 12 is about $54 a month. The table gives no period, so treat this as approximate.

High end, tank plus hauled water

Documented Appendix N (page 83) states an average annual cost of $13,000 per household for bottled or hauled water. $13,000 / 12 is about $1,083 a month.

Disclosed discrepancy

Derived Table N-1 lists $15,027,287 for 529 hauled-water households, about $28,400 each, which does not reconcile with the $13,000 average, and the table does not state periods. The series uses $13,000 as the State's stated average.

Cross-checks

  • The State's model for bottled water is about $97 a month (stage 3).
  • Privately bought trucked water runs $200 to $350 a month (stage 3).
  • Duration varies. Self-Help Enterprises says participants may stay on the tank program about a year; East Orosi relied on State-supplied water for 14 years.
  • Not every household in the dry-well count gets interim help (renters, people who never apply), so the range applies only if the State steps in.

5. Resolved: about $54,000 per well, usually state-funded

  • Documented Appendix N (page 83) gives $54,000 as the average cost of constructing a new domestic well, in Self-Help Enterprises' state-funded program.
  • Derived Table N-1 lists 196 wells drilled for $10,486,906, about $53,500 each.

Cross-checks

  • Derived The State's cost model for a 500-foot private well uses $65 a foot ($32,500 of drilling). The itemised equation sums to about $46,000 before the county permit and 3.1% inflation. Self-Help Enterprises' reported average drilling cost in the model is $31,250.
  • Resident and program quotes: $35,000 and $55,000 (Food & Water Watch), $48,000 or more for a 420-foot well and pump (RCAC), and $85,000 for an 800-foot well in San Luis Obispo County (SLO Tribune via Yahoo).
  • Household financing is capped well below these costs: USDA household well loans cap at $11,000, and RCAC grants can reach $60,000.

6. What the cost slide leaves out

Connection to a public water system (consolidation) is omitted from the headline number because it is priced per connection on whole-system projects, not per household bill:

  • Regional approaches: $7,000 to $39,000 per connection (Monterey County, 85 small systems; The Revelator).
  • Derived East Porterville: $48 million for 755 homes, about $63,600 each (DWR).
  • State average for water-system construction: $69,500 per connection (FY 2026-27 Fund Expenditure Plan, Table C-7), covering all construction types, not only consolidation.
  • Derived East Orosi, an outlier: $13.5 million for about 103 connections, about $131,000 each (State Water Board order and Resolution 2024-0008).

The series does not estimate a total or annual public cost of groundwater depletion. Costs such as subsidence damage to infrastructure, property values and pumping energy are not quantified here.
