Shifting Baselines and the Elusiveness of Climate Change

Why does gradual climate change go unnoticed? The piece compares what each generation in Germany has experienced as normal: hot days per year rose from about 4 for Baby Boomers to about 9 for Gen Z, and frost days fell by about 16 over the period.

Published
Earth.Org, 14 April 2021
My role
Data Journalism Team Lead (2020 to 2022)
Tools
Python and Adobe Illustrator

What it shows

Why does gradual climate change go unnoticed? The piece compares what each generation in Germany has experienced as normal: hot days per year rose from about 4 for Baby Boomers to about 9 for Gen Z, and frost days fell by about 16 over the period.

What I did

Data Journalism Team Lead at Earth.Org (January 2020 to January 2022), leading a volunteer data team of between 5 and 15 people. For each piece I conceptualised it, did the research and directed the number crunching and the final design. A graphic designer handled final production. Data analysis by Daniel Müller-Komorowska.

Data and methods

Daily weather records from the German weather service since 1946. Hot days are days at or above 30°C and frost days are days with a minimum below 0°C, averaged over the early life of each generation (Baby Boomers, Gen X, Millennials, Gen Z).

Full sources are listed in the original article.

Tools

Python and Adobe Illustrator.

Who I worked with

Earth.Org's data team was made up of volunteer contributors from around the world, between 5 and 15 people at a time. Data analysis by Daniel Müller-Komorowska.

Limits

The article says plainly that one country and one metric over a short period cannot demonstrate climate change on their own. It illustrates a perception effect that the wider research has established.
